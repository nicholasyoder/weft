# Neural World Models and Generative Tooling as Prior Art for Agentic Game Engines

Research note for the Weft project. Covers neural "world model" game generators, commentary on whether they can replace traditional engines, projects combining LLM agents with world models, and adjacent generative tooling (3D asset generation, MCP integrations for existing engines).

---

## 1. Neural world models / generative game engines

### Google DeepMind — Genie 1 / 2 / 3
- **What it is**: A family of "foundation world models" that generate interactive, playable environments frame-by-frame from a prompt, with no underlying game engine, physics engine, or 3D representation.
- **Evolution**: Genie 1 (March 2024) generated 2D playable environments from a single image, action space learned unsupervised from unlabeled internet video. Genie 2 (Dec 2024) jumped to 3D worlds with emergent physics/character animation but was never accompanied by a full paper (research demo only). Genie 3 (Aug 2025) is real-time (24 fps), 720p, holds consistency for "a few minutes," and lets a user issue new text prompts mid-simulation to alter the world on the fly.
- **Architecture (high level)**: Trained on large video corpora; internally it's an autoregressive/diffusion-style video model conditioned on a learned discrete latent action space and past frames — DeepMind has not published Genie 3's architecture in detail, unlike the original Genie 1 paper.
- **Latency/real-time**: Genie 3 runs live at 24 fps; earlier versions were slower and often used offline/batch generation.
- **Maturity & control interface**: Restricted research preview through most of 2025; DeepMind opened a limited public demo ("Project Genie") in January 2026. As of this research there is **no public production API** — third-party sites advertising a "Genie 3 API" are not official. The one clear agentic hook that does exist: DeepMind explicitly pairs Genie with **SIMA / SIMA 2**, a Gemini-powered embodied agent that is trained and evaluated by acting *inside* Genie-generated worlds — i.e., Genie is used today as an infinite, on-demand training/eval environment generator for RL/embodied agents, not as a scriptable content backend for game designers.
- Sources: [DeepMind Genie 3 blog](https://deepmind.google/blog/genie-3-a-new-frontier-for-world-models/), [Genie model page](https://deepmind.google/models/genie/), [TechCrunch on Genie 3](https://techcrunch.com/2025/08/05/deepmind-thinks-genie-3-world-model-presents-stepping-stone-towards-agi/), [Genie (world model) — Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Genie_(world_model)), [Genie 2 blog](https://deepmind.google/blog/genie-2-a-large-scale-foundation-world-model/), [SIMA 2 blog](https://deepmind.google/blog/sima-2-an-agent-that-plays-reasons-and-learns-with-you-in-virtual-3d-worlds/), [MIT Tech Review on SIMA/Genie training](https://www.technologyreview.com/2025/11/13/1127921/google-deepmind-is-using-gemini-to-train-agents-inside-goat-simulator-3/).

### GameNGen (Google/academic research, 2024)
- **What it is**: The first "neural game engine" — DOOM rendered entirely by a diffusion model, no game code or renderer involved at inference time.
- **Architecture**: Two-phase training — (1) an RL agent plays DOOM and its play is recorded to build a dataset of (frame, action) trajectories; (2) a diffusion model is trained to predict the next frame conditioned on the history of past frames and actions ("Diffusion Models Are Real-Time Game Engines").
- **Performance**: ~20 fps on a single TPU, stable for multi-minute sessions, next-frame PSNR of 29.4 (comparable to lossy JPEG), and human raters were barely better than chance at telling real DOOM from the simulation in short clips.
- **Maturity & control interface**: Pure research artifact — a paper and project page, not a product or API. No mechanism for an agent to *author* new game content; it can only reproduce a game it was trained on. Important precedent for "what's the theoretical ceiling," not something buildable on today.
- Sources: [arXiv:2408.14837](https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.14837), [GameNGen project page](https://gamengen.github.io/).

### DIAMOND (Diffusion As a Model Of eNvironment Dreams, 2024)
- **What it is**: A world model used as an RL *training substrate* rather than a playable artifact for humans — an agent is trained entirely inside a diffusion-generated Atari/CS:GO simulation.
- **Architecture**: Replaces the discrete-latent world models common in Dreamer-style RL (which lose visual detail) with a diffusion model that predicts future frames directly in pixel space, preserving small but gameplay-relevant details. Standard train loop: collect real trajectories → train the diffusion world model on them → train a policy by RL entirely inside the learned world model → collect more real data with the improved policy → repeat.
- **Performance**: State-of-the-art mean human-normalized score (1.46) on the Atari 100k benchmark for agents trained purely inside a world model; also demonstrated on a CS:GO-like environment.
- **Maturity & control interface**: Research codebase (open-sourced), used as an RL research tool, not a content-creation or agent-driven design tool. Relevant mainly to the "simulation backend for training agents" idea, not to a human/agent building a shippable game.
- Sources: [arXiv:2405.12399](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.12399), [NeurIPS 2024 poster](https://neurips.cc/virtual/2024/poster/95428).

### Decart — Oasis / Oasis 3 / Lucy
- **What it is**: "The world's first real-time AI world model" — an open-weights (500M parameter checkpoint released) Minecraft-like playable diffusion model, built by Decart with Etched. A larger, closed checkpoint is demoed live.
- **Architecture**: ViT-based spatial autoencoder + a Diffusion Transformer (DiT) latent diffusion backbone, trained with "Diffusion Forcing," with temporal attention layers interleaved with spatial attention to carry context across frames — no physics engine, no game code, "just a foundation model" reacting to keyboard/mouse input.
- **Latency**: ~20 fps on the original Oasis; Decart's newer "Lucy" model claims sub-30ms response latency, positioned as a general "world model for immersive experiences" (gaming, virtual try-on, livestreaming, ad insertion).
- **Maturity & commercial trajectory**: Decart has moved fastest toward commercialization of any lab in this space — $300M raise (May 2026, ~$4B valuation, investors incl. Nvidia, Sequoia, Adobe Ventures), a partnership with Comcast/NVIDIA to deploy Lucy on distributed infrastructure, and "Oasis 3" pushing into photorealistic, longer-duration generation (including a driving-simulation demo covered skeptically by TechCrunch: "can simulate hours of photorealistic driving — with some caveats"). Still no evidence of a documented, stable content-authoring API for developers to script game logic; interaction is limited to raw input signals (keys/mouse) and occasionally text prompts to "remix" the world.
- Sources: [Oasis project page](https://oasis-model.github.io/), [Decart.ai](https://decart.ai/), [Tom's Guide on Oasis](https://www.tomsguide.com/ai/ive-seen-the-future-of-gaming-ai-model-creates-playable-minecraft-from-a-picture), [Decart $300M raise](https://theaiinsider.tech/2026/05/25/decart-secures-300m-to-power-real-time-ai-with-world-models-and-next-gen-inference-stack/), [TechCrunch on Decart driving sim](https://techcrunch.com/2026/06/10/decarts-new-world-model-can-simulate-hours-of-photorealistic-driving-with-some-caveats/).

### World Labs (Fei-Fei Li) — Marble
- **What it is**: Distinct from the "live, streaming" world models above — Marble generates **persistent, exportable 3D environments** (Gaussian splats, meshes, or video) from text/image/video input, rather than an endless on-the-fly video stream. Launched as World Labs' first commercial product (Nov 2025).
- **Architecture/approach**: Decouples structure from style — "Chisel," an experimental 3D editor, lets a user block out a coarse spatial layout, then a generative model fills in visual style/detail conditioned on that layout, producing a real, geometry-backed 3D scene rather than a pure video hallucination.
- **Relevance to agents**: Because outputs are actual exportable 3D assets (splats/meshes) rather than raw video, Marble is the closest thing in this list to something a traditional engine (or an agent scripting one) could actually **import and use** — e.g., generate a level's environment art or a explorable backdrop, then wire up gameplay logic conventionally on top. World Labs frames this as one of "three types" of world model (generation vs. reconstruction vs. simulation), explicitly positioning Marble as environment/asset generation rather than a full simulator.
- **Maturity**: Commercial product with a web app; still early, geometry/consistency artifacts are common, and it is not a real-time interactive "play inside it" experience — outputs are meant to be exported and used elsewhere.
- Sources: [World Labs Marble blog](https://www.worldlabs.ai/blog/marble-world-model), [TechCrunch on Marble launch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/11/12/fei-fei-lis-world-labs-speeds-up-the-world-model-race-with-marble-its-first-commercial-product/), [Fast Company](https://www.fastcompany.com/91437004/fei-fei-li-world-labs-spatial-ai-mapping-3d), [Tech Times on World Labs' 3-type taxonomy](https://www.techtimes.com/articles/317927/20260606/feifei-lis-world-labs-splits-world-model-three-types-marble-targets-simulation-linchpin.htm).

### Microsoft Research / Xbox / Ninja Theory — Muse (WHAM)
- **What it is**: "World and Human Action Model" trained on ~1 billion images and controller actions from ~7 years of recorded *Bleeding Edge* gameplay (one year of anonymized play from ~28,000 players).
- **Architecture**: Jointly generates visual frame continuations *and* the corresponding controller actions, i.e. it models the joint distribution of (game state, visuals, player input) rather than just visuals conditioned on input.
- **Positioning**: Explicitly pitched for **gameplay ideation and preservation**, not as a replacement engine — Microsoft's own framing is "feed in a starting state, then explore/branch/edit alternative continuations" via the "WHAM Demonstrator," aimed at game designers exploring "what if" variations of an existing game, and at preserving/reviving old game experiences.
- **Maturity & control interface**: Model weights, sample data, and the WHAM Demonstrator tool are released on Azure AI Foundry / Hugging Face — this is the most "available" of the closed-studio world models, but it is trained on a single specific game (Bleeding Edge) and does not generalize to arbitrary new games without retraining on that game's data. Not agent-controllable via a general API in any documented way beyond the demonstrator UI.
- Sources: [Xbox Wire announcement](https://news.xbox.com/en-us/2025/02/19/muse-ai-xbox-empowering-creators-and-players/), [Microsoft Research blog](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/blog/introducing-muse-our-first-generative-ai-model-designed-for-gameplay-ideation/), [microsoft/wham on Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/microsoft/wham).

### NVIDIA — Cosmos and ACE (adjacent, not gaming-first)
- **Cosmos**: A platform of "world foundation models" (WFMs) plus tokenizers/data pipelines, aimed primarily at **robotics and autonomous-vehicle physical AI**, not consumer games. Its game-relevant piece is "sim-to-real" upscaling — taking a low-fidelity game-engine or physics-simulator (e.g. Isaac Sim) render and using a generative model to add realistic textures/lighting on top, i.e. generative post-processing of a conventional engine's output rather than replacing the engine. Cosmos 3 (2026) is built on a mixture-of-transformers architecture unifying vision reasoning, world generation, and action prediction.
- **NVIDIA ACE**: Not a world model at all — a suite of on-device/cloud AI microservices (speech, LLM-driven dialogue via NeMo, facial animation via Audio2Face) for giving **NPCs** natural-language conversation and increasingly "agentic" perceive-plan-act behavior, deployed inside conventional engines (Unreal/Unity titles from PUBG, NetEase, miHoYo, Ubisoft, etc.). Directly relevant as prior art for "LLM-driven game agents living inside a traditional engine," which is a much more mature, shipped pattern than full neural world models.
- Sources: [NVIDIA Cosmos](https://www.nvidia.com/en-us/ai/cosmos/), [MindStudio on Cosmos 3](https://www.mindstudio.ai/blog/what-is-nvidia-cosmos-3-world-foundation-model), [NVIDIA ACE for Games](https://www.nvidia.com/en-us/geforce/news/nvidia-ace-for-games-generative-ai-npcs/), [NVIDIA ACE autonomous characters](https://www.nvidia.com/en-us/geforce/news/nvidia-ace-autonomous-ai-companions-pubg-naraka-bladepoint/).

### Runway — Game Worlds (beta)
- **What it is**: A consumer-facing product (beta, Aug 2025) offering preset and custom "living, interactive worlds" generated in real time from prompts — narrative games (a heist story, a mystery, a history piece) where "worlds, characters and storylines aren't rendered, modeled, or scripted, but are imagined, formed and generated as you play."
- **Relevance**: This is closer to interactive-fiction-with-generated-imagery than a true first-person playable world model like Genie/Oasis; it demonstrates the productization end of the spectrum — a consumer can already "play" a Runway-generated narrative game today, but the interaction model is closer to branching narrative + generated stills/video than free-form physical action in a persistent 3D space.
- Sources: [Runway Research: Game Worlds](https://runwayml.com/research/runway-game-worlds), [Medium coverage](https://medium.com/kinomoto-mag/runway-launches-game-worlds-beta-07428f25a0f8).

### Odyssey
- **What it is**: London startup (founded by ex-self-driving engineers) building an autoregressive world model that streams a new frame every ~40ms based on current scene + user input + history, targeting film and game production first, with an explicit stated ambition of becoming "a training ground for agentic AI" letting agents learn by acting inside simulated worlds.
- **Maturity**: Research preview / demo, $27M raised, notable advisor (Ed Catmull, Pixar co-founder) signaling ambitions toward production-quality media, not just tech demos.
- Sources: [TechCrunch on Odyssey](https://techcrunch.com/2025/05/28/odysseys-new-ai-model-streams-3d-interactive-worlds), [the-decoder.com](https://the-decoder.com/generative-ai-startup-odyssey-demos-interactive-ai-generated-video/).

## 2. Commentary: can world models replace traditional game engines?

The clearest, most concrete signal available is that **the leading practical answer, as of 2026, is "hollow out the engine, don't replace it."** Roblox's own public architecture announcement is the best evidence: their "Reality Hybrid Architecture" (April 2026) keeps a conventional game engine in charge of *state* — collision, physics, object positions/velocities, long-term memory, symbolic game logic, multiplayer consistency — while a post-trained **Video World Model** is layered on top purely to generate textures, lighting, and fine-scale visual dynamics. Movement of a car, for instance, is computed by the engine; the World Model only decides how it *looks*. ([Roblox announcement](https://about.roblox.com/newsroom/2026/04/roblox-reality-hybrid-architecture-democratizing-photorealistic-multiplayer-gaming))

This "engine-plus-generative-renderer" pattern recurs across the research literature:
- A 2026 analysis ("From Pixels to States: Rethinking Interactive World Models as Game Engines," [arXiv:2607.14076](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.14076)) frames the comparison along four axes — player action control, game-state dynamics, state-observation persistence, and real-time generation — and contrasts video generative models (which merely *predict future observations*) against conventional engines (which maintain explicit, queryable state and apply rules deterministically). The implicit critique: a pure video-generation loop has no ground-truth state to check consistency against.
- A capability-audit paper, "From Generation to Simulation: How Far Are World Models from Being True Simulators?" ([arXiv:2608.23070](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.23070)), concludes current world models achieve *functional* substitution for interaction/controllability in narrow scenarios but fall short of the formal physics guarantees a real simulator provides, and that richer simulation typically leans on exported representations (e.g. Gaussian splats, as in Marble) governed by a conventional physics engine — geometry is learned, dynamics are prescribed.
- A widely-cited practitioner critique (Gradient Flow newsletter, ["World Model" is a mess"](https://gradientflow.substack.com/p/world-model-is-a-mess-heres-how-to)) argues the term has become an overloaded marketing umbrella covering wildly different systems (video generators, robotics simulators, RL latent-dynamics models, LLM-based text simulators), and that practical adoption requires separating "what generates pixels" from "what tracks ground truth."
- The recurring list of unresolved practical problems across this literature: **determinism, replayability, object permanence, multiplayer consistency, debuggability, physics fidelity, latency, and inference cost.** These are precisely the properties a shippable game (and an agent iterating on one) depends on, and they are also precisely what pure neural world models are weakest at.
- On the rendering-speed side specifically, "Generative World Renderer at the Speed of Play" ([arXiv:2607.18703](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.18703)) reports pushing a generative forward-renderer (AlayaRenderer-Flash) from 0.56 fps to 31.54 fps by conditioning generation on a G-buffer from a conventional renderer — again the pattern of "engine computes structure, generative model computes final pixels."

**Bottom line for this project**: credible technical opinion converges on augmentation (generative rendering/asset layer on top of a deterministic simulation core), not replacement. Full "pure neural" engines (GameNGen, Oasis, Genie) remain valuable as research signals of ceiling capability and as training-environment generators for embodied agents, but nobody serious is proposing to ship a multiplayer, debuggable, agent-editable game purely as an autoregressive video model in the near term.

## 3. LLM agents driving world models / generative engines

Two distinct patterns show up in current research, and they are architecturally very different:

1. **LLM agent as controller of a neural (pixel-space) world model.** DeepMind's SIMA/SIMA 2 + Genie 3 pairing is the most concrete real example: a Gemini-powered agent perceives, reasons about, and acts inside worlds Genie generates on demand, effectively using the world model as an infinite procedurally-generated training/eval gym. This is agent-*uses*-world-model, not agent-*authors*-game-content-via-world-model — there's no "agent writes a spec, world model renders the whole game" pipeline shipping anywhere yet.

2. **LLM agent as the simulation substrate itself (text/structured, not pixels).** This is a more mature and immediately relevant pattern for an agent-native engine:
   - "Orchestrated Reality: From Role-Play to Living, Playable Game Worlds — LLM-Driven World Simulation as a Parameterized-Action POMDP" ([arXiv:2606.16014](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.16014)) frames the whole game-world simulation problem as a POMDP where the **world state is a JSON object**, actions are a defined parameterized action space, and an LLM plays the role of both the observation model (rendering text/descriptions of the state) and the transition function (deciding how actions change the JSON state). This is not neural pixel rendering at all — it's the LLM acting as a structured, inspectable simulation engine, which is far closer to something a code-first agentic engine could actually adopt (e.g., LLM-authored/updated JSON game state + a conventional renderer, rather than an LLM hallucinating pixels).
   - Related work explicitly pushes toward LLM-generated **executable code** as the world model (so transitions are deterministic and formally verifiable) or LLM-driven **validation pipelines** over structured JSON state machines, rather than a learned neural network for world dynamics — directly analogous to "LLM writes/edits game logic code," which is the actual practice already used by AI coding agents today, just formalized as a research pattern.
   - Multi-agent "build-the-world-model" frameworks like **AGENT2WORLD** coordinate several LLM agents (research → model development → testing) to iteratively produce an executable world model — a genuinely agentic-engineering pattern (agents building simulation code, not agents prompting a video model), directly relevant as prior art for "AI agents building a game engine."
   - Earlier/adjacent systems: **CALYPSO** (LLM agent assisting a Dungeon Master with narrative generation for D&D) and **GameGPT** (dual-agent, hierarchical LLM pipeline automating parts of game *development*, not just runtime play) are useful precedents for LLM agents operating on the production/tooling side rather than the runtime-rendering side.
   - Sources: [arXiv:2606.16014](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.16014), survey context in [arXiv:2503.21460](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2503.21460), [PIANIST arXiv:2411.15998](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2411.15998).

**Takeaway**: the "agent + world model" combination that's actually being explored seriously (POMDP/JSON-state approaches, code-generating world models) looks much more like *conventional software with an LLM-authored simulation layer* than like an agent steering a diffusion video model. This strongly favors a code-first, state-first architecture for an agent-native engine, with generative media relegated to an asset/content layer rather than the runtime truth source.

## 4. Adjacent generative tooling: PCG research, 3D asset generation, and MCP integrations

### Procedural content generation (PCG) research trend
A 2024 survey, "Procedural Content Generation in Games: A Survey with Insights on Emerging LLM Integration" ([arXiv:2410.15644](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.15644)), reviewing 207 papers (2019–2023) across PCG venues (FDG, CoG, AIIDE, CHI-PLAY), finds the field's trajectory has been search-based methods → ML-based methods (including PCGRL, i.e. reinforcement-learning-driven level generation) → and now increasingly hybrid approaches combining GANs/RL/LLMs. The clear direction: LLMs are being layered on top of, not replacing, older PCG techniques, generating higher-level design intent that's then executed by more classical generators — again a "generative layer over deterministic system" pattern.

### 3D/texture asset generation (usable *today* in an agent pipeline)
These are the most immediately actionable tools for a practical agentic engine, since they produce real importable assets (meshes, textures, rigs) rather than a whole-game video stream:
- **Meshy** — text-to-3D and image-to-3D with built-in auto-rigging and 500+ animation presets; has a documented API ([meshy.ai/api](https://www.meshy.ai/api)) suitable for an agent to call programmatically to generate a mesh + texture + rig from a text description, roughly a minute per asset.
- **Tripo3D** — text/image/multiview-to-3D, faster (avg. ~8s per model) with retopology and auto-rigging, developer API at [developers.tripo3d.ai](https://developers.tripo3d.ai/en); lighter export options on lower tiers.
- **Luma AI** — differentiator is real-world capture (phone-based Gaussian Splatting/NeRF scanning) combined with generation; outputs often need retopology before game-engine use, less turnkey than Meshy/Tripo for a fully automated pipeline.
- All three are plausible building blocks for an agent-driven "asset factory" step in a practical engine (agent decides it needs a prop/character mesh, calls the API, imports the result), which is a much nearer-term win than anything in the world-model section above.
- Sources: [Meshy comparison blog](https://www.meshy.ai/blog/best-ai-tools-for-3d-game-assets), [RapidDirect 2026 comparison](https://www.rapiddirect.com/blog/best-8-ai-3d-model-generators/).

### MCP servers / agent tool-use integrations for existing engines (directly relevant, and maturing fast)
This is arguably the most directly transferable prior art for "what does an agent-native engine's tool surface look like," because it already exists and works today against mainstream engines:
- **Blender MCP** ([github.com/ahujasid/blender-mcp](https://github.com/ahujasid/blender-mcp)) — a Blender addon runs a local socket server; an MCP server bridges an LLM (Claude, others) to Blender's Python API, letting an agent create/modify/delete objects, apply materials, set up lighting/cameras, pull assets from Poly Haven, generate meshes via Hyper3D Rodin, run arbitrary Python in Blender, and export scene data. Notably, Anthropic has since added first-class Blender support in "Claude for Creative Work" and joined the Blender Development Fund as a corporate patron — signaling this pattern is being invested in officially, not just a community hack.
- **Unity MCP** ([github.com/CoplayDev/unity-mcp](https://github.com/CoplayDev/unity-mcp)) — MIT-licensed bridge giving agents (Claude Code, Cursor, VS Code Copilot, etc.) live access to a running Unity Editor: inspect scenes, read console output, edit scripts, trigger editor actions, manage assets. Unity itself has since shipped an MCP server as part of its own AI tooling (beta).
- **Unreal MCP** — Epic shipped a first-party, in-editor MCP server as an *experimental* feature in Unreal Engine 5.8, letting any MCP-compatible agent spawn actors, configure lighting, create material instances, inspect UI widgets, run automation tests, and call studio-defined custom tools. Epic went further and shipped Unreal MCP inside UEFN (Fortnite's creator tools) in August 2026, letting agents write Verse code, place devices, edit the scene graph, and debug play sessions directly. This makes Unreal Engine the first AAA engine to ship agent tooling from the engine vendor itself (vs. Unity/Blender's community-driven integrations).
- **Relevance**: this is the strongest concrete evidence that "agent drives a real engine via a structured tool protocol" is already validated, shipping technology — not speculative. A new agent-native engine built from scratch should assume MCP (or an equivalent structured tool-call protocol) as the baseline interface an agent uses to manipulate the engine, and can look at Unreal's tool surface (spawn/configure/inspect/test/custom-tool) as a reference shape for what such a tool API needs to cover.
- Sources: [Blender MCP GitHub](https://github.com/ahujasid/blender-mcp), [MindStudio on Claude+Blender](https://www.mindstudio.ai/blog/claude-blender-mcp-real-world-performance), [Unity MCP GitHub](https://github.com/CoplayDev/unity-mcp), [Unity's own MCP blog](https://unity.com/blog/unity-ai-mcp-how-to-get-started), [Unreal MCP in UE 5.8 coverage](https://byteiota.com/unreal-engine-5-8-ships-mcp-server-ai-agents-can-now-drive-the-editor/), [Unreal MCP in UEFN coverage](https://explainx.ai/blog/uefn-unreal-mcp-fortnite-verse-agents-august-2026).

## Implications for a practical, code-first agentic game engine

**What's genuinely borrowable now:**
1. **The MCP tool-surface pattern is the most important transferable idea here.** Blender/Unity/Unreal all converge on the same shape: a structured protocol exposing "inspect state," "mutate state (spawn/edit/delete objects, materials, scripts)," "run arbitrary code," and "call custom studio-defined tools." A from-scratch agentic engine should design its native API to be this kind of tool surface from day one, rather than bolting an MCP wrapper onto a GUI-first engine later. Unreal's tool taxonomy (spawn, configure, inspect, test, custom) is a reasonable starting checklist.
2. **The "hollow-out-the-engine" hybrid pattern (Roblox's own architecture, the G-buffer-conditioned renderer research) is the right long-term slot for generative AI in a new engine** — keep an explicit, deterministic, inspectable state/physics/logic layer (so an agent can reason about, test, and debug it, and so multiplayer/replay/determinism work at all), and treat generative models strictly as an optional rendering/content layer on top, never as the source of truth.
3. **3D asset-generation APIs (Meshy, Tripo3D) are ready to integrate today** as a content-authoring tool an agent can call — this is low-risk, high-value, and worth prototyping early (agent describes an asset, calls the API, imports a mesh/texture/rig into the engine's asset pipeline).
4. **LLM-as-structured-simulation (POMDP/JSON-state, code-generating world models) is a much better mental model than "LLM drives a video model"** for anything resembling actual gameplay logic — an agent-native engine's "world model," if it has one, should look like agent-authored/edited game-state code and data, not pixel generation.
5. **NPC/dialogue-agent patterns (NVIDIA ACE) are mature and shippable** and are a legitimate near-term use of generative AI *inside* a conventional engine (character behavior/dialogue), independent of the whole-world-model question.

**What's speculative / longer-term, and why:**
1. **Pure neural world models (Genie, Oasis, GameNGen, Odyssey) are not viable as an engine's runtime today** — no determinism, no reliable object permanence, no debuggability, no multiplayer consistency, high inference cost, and (except Decart's aggressive commercialization push) largely closed research previews with no stable APIs. They're valuable as a north star / stress test for "what will eventually be possible," and as training-environment generators for embodied/RL agents (the SIMA+Genie pattern), but not as something to architect a 2026 practical engine around.
2. **"Agent writes a game spec, world model renders the whole game" is not a demonstrated working pipeline anywhere** in this research — the closest real analogues (Orchestrated Reality's POMDP framing, AGENT2WORLD) generate structured/code simulations, not full neural renders, which reinforces that this idea is presently better realized as "agent writes code for a real engine" than "agent prompts a video model."
3. **Marble-style persistent 3D generation (splats/meshes) is promising as a future environment-authoring shortcut** but is still early enough (geometry/consistency artifacts, non-real-time) that it belongs in a "watch and revisit" category rather than something to build a dependency on now.
